Education Labs at Anthropic

Three proposals for how Claude could strengthen STEM teacher training in low-income schools — drawn from my field research on the Agastya model in Mumbai's municipal schools and from products I've shipped that use AI to teach and transfer expert knowledge.

The premise, from my research, is that the binding constraint in low-income STEM classrooms isn't student ability — it's teacher capacity. There are too few well-trained STEM teachers, they carry impossible loads, and they rarely get coaching. The highest-leverage place for AI is not replacing the teacher but multiplying a good one. Each idea below is a lab that trains, coaches, or amplifies teachers rather than routing around them.

1 · The STEM Co-Teacher

An AI teacher-training companion for under-resourced STEM educators

  • Problem: Municipal-school teachers are often assigned STEM subjects outside their training and get little ongoing support, so lessons default to rote memorization
  • What Claude does: Generates syllabus-aligned, hands-on lesson plans; models Socratic questioning the teacher can practice against; and offers just-in-time coaching ("a student is stuck on fractions — here are three concrete ways to reteach it")
  • Grounded in my research: Directly operationalizes Agastya's train-the-trainer model, which I studied in the field — it scales the human, inquiry-driven core rather than replacing it
  • Pilot: A cohort of BMC teachers in Dadar, Mahim, and Colaba, measuring change in lesson quality, teacher confidence, and student engagement over one term

2 · The Low-Bandwidth Tutor

A multilingual, adaptive STEM tutor that reaches students where they already are

  • Problem: One teacher can't give 60 students one-on-one time, and most edtech assumes reliable devices and bandwidth that these classrooms don't have
  • What Claude does: Delivers patient, one-on-one explanation over SMS and WhatsApp in a student's own language (Hindi, Marathi), adapting to each learner's pace — and gives the teacher a dashboard of where the class is struggling
  • Grounded in my work: I've shipped exactly this delivery model — Pinch runs a conversational AI over Twilio SMS and WhatsApp Business to ~200 weekly users, and my AI-native curriculum paper argues low-bandwidth tutors can partially close the teacher-shortage gap
  • Pilot: Supplement one teacher's classroom with after-hours tutoring, measuring homework completion and concept retention against a control section

3 · The Master-Teacher Amplifier

Capture what great teachers do, then make it reusable feedback at scale

  • Problem: A school's best teachers are a scarce, non-scalable resource, and grading plus feedback eat the hours teachers would rather spend teaching
  • What Claude does: Captures a master teacher's explanations and worked examples into reusable teaching assets, and drafts individualized feedback on student work for the teacher to approve — keeping a human in the loop
  • Grounded in my work: Mirrors Skinopsis, where I built AI to capture doctors' spoken expertise into structured output, and my TA work automating grading to cut turnaround by 70% so feedback reached students faster
  • Pilot: Deploy in a teacher-scarce subject, measuring hours returned to teachers and speed and quality of feedback students receive

Across all three, the through-line is the same conviction from my research and my products: AI's role in education is to make one good teacher reach many, especially where teachers are scarcest.
